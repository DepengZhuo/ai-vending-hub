Smart Locker vs. Smart Cooler: Which Unattended Format Fits?

Updated August 2026 · Editorial Team · 6 min read

Smart lockers excel at controlled, pre-ordered pickup; smart coolers win on browsing and impulse sales. Choose by product, not by hype.

Plain-English analogy: Think of it as a mailbox versus a fridge: a locker hands you a specific item behind a door, while a cooler lets you browse an open shelf and take what you want. The two formats exist to solve different problems - controlled pickup versus spontaneous snack runs.

TL;DR

Smart lockers charge after a compartment closes, which is precise but requires a selection step. Smart coolers let shoppers browse open shelves, which drives impulse buys but needs strong vision recognition.

What is a smart locker?

A smart locker is a cabinet of individually locked compartments. The customer scans to open a specific compartment, takes the item, and closes it. Payment happens on close, so recognition is nearly perfect.

What is a smart cooler?

A smart cooler is an open-door fridge with computer vision. The customer can pick multiple items and return items, and the cameras build the basket. It is faster but depends on recognition quality.

Where lockers win

  • Pre-ordered meals and click-and-collect.
  • High-value or age-restricted products.
  • Locations where exact attribution matters more than browsing.

Where coolers win

  • Break rooms, gyms, and lobbies where impulse sales drive revenue.
  • Fresh food and drinks with mixed baskets.
  • Locations that need no selection UI at all.

Frequently asked questions

Can a smart locker sell multiple items?

Yes, but the customer must select each item. It is slower for multi-item baskets.

Which format has lower shrink?

Lockers have near-zero shrink because compartments close; coolers depend on recognition and camera coverage.

Can I run both formats together?

Yes. Many operators place a cooler for impulse sales and lockers for pre-orders in the same location.
